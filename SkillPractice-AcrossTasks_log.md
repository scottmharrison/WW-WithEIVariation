## 2026-11-07: Balanced-input control signal (Lo, Wang & Wang, 2015), biophysical version — validation

- **Decision (Scott).** Build the biophysical version of the paper's balanced-synaptic-input (BSI) control signal (strength S, ratio R), add accuracy fitting, and run the 16 contrasts; explain first how it differs from the paper and how accuracy would work in StarCraft 2.
- **Measured.** The paper's ratio-to-balance-potential relation R = 0.4 (0 − V_B)/(V_B + 70) reproduces both of its reported pairs (1.156 ↔ −52 mV, 1.297 ↔ −53.5 mV) (`model/bsi_transfer.py`).
- **Measured.** Mean-field (Siegert) transfer function of the paper's excitatory neuron, noise fitted: closest to the reduced model's phi at sigma = 2.64 mV, RMS 2.49 Hz over 0-60 Hz, derived (a, b, d) = (313, 128, 0.203) against (270, 108, 0.154) (`process/output/check_03.txt`). With the BSI added in that reduction the low-rate slope changes the same way for R = 1.114 and R = 1.247 (+13.8% and +14.0% at S = 0.5, conductance scale 3 nS per unit S, noise scaled with conductance; −15.4% and −16.1% with noise held fixed) (same file and session output).
- **Measured.** Direct simulation of the paper's conductance-based neuron (2,400 Hz background at 2.1 nS, BSI Poisson spikes at 0.1 nS, 10 s per point, common random numbers, seed 20261107), assuming S = 0.3 r_e[kHz] g[nS]: slope at 5 Hz changes by −0.5% to −9.3% at S = 0.5 for all four ratios tested; the ratio mainly shifts the curve (offset b −27.3 → −18.2 at R = 1.297) (`process/output/check_04.txt`). At 3x that conductance: −11.6% (R = 1.114) and −13.5% (R = 1.297), offset −25.5 and +4.7; at 10x: −25.5% and −29.3% (`process/output/check_04b.txt`).
- **Inferred.** In the paper's single neuron, simulated in isolation, BSI is a divisive gain reduction whose size matches the paper's 10-15% at about 3x the assumed units, plus a shift of the curve set by the ratio; the gain increase the paper reports for R ≤ 1.156 is not reproduced. Alternatives: the paper's f-I axis is presynaptic input rate rather than injected current; its neurons sit in a network state (around −53 mV, per its Methods) where V_B = −52 mV is depolarizing, which an isolated neuron at its own rest does not reproduce; the units of S differ (defined in Wang et al., 2013, not read). Not separated.
- **Flag.** Not run: the 16-contrast fits with the control signal, pending Scott's choice of how to proceed; the version that can be built now is not the paper's mechanism in its ratio-dependent gain.
- **Measured.** Wang, Lee, Wang & Lo (2013, PLoS ONE 8:e62379, PMC3633869), read via the PMC page: control populations of 500 neurons, AMPA 0.1 nS onto the selective cells, S = 0.3 r_e g with r_e the rate of one control neuron in Hz (its r_e = 67.2 Hz ↔ S ≈ 2.0; 0.3 x 67.2 x 0.1 = 2.02); GABA conductance variable; critical ratio about 1.156 (feedforward BSI). It reports no single-neuron f-I curve; its account of the effect is a network energy landscape (crater around the undecided state; performance linear in the wall-height difference).
- **Decided by Claude.** Strength units corrected: mean excitatory control conductance = 500 x 0.1 nS x 2 ms x r_e = 3.33 S nS, half the 6.67 S nS assumed in check_04.
- **Measured.** With corrected units, input axis = extra excitatory Poisson rate (2.1 nS AMPA), same control rate for both populations and GABA conductance R x 0.1 nS: slope at 5 Hz falls at every ratio (S = 0.5: −6.4% to −8.8%; S = 1.0: −1.5% to −13.5%; S = 2.0: −8.8% to −28.0%), more with higher R; output at 1.5 kHz input rises for R ≤ 1.0-1.114 and falls for R ≥ 1.156 (S = 1.0: 42.4, 37.4, 35.1 Hz at R = 1.0, 1.114, 1.156 against 37.3 baseline) (`process/output/check_05.txt`, seed 20261107).
- **Inferred.** In the paper's neuron in isolation, BSI is a divisive gain reduction that grows with S and R, plus a net shift whose sign changes near R = 1.11-1.16, close to the 2013 paper's critical ratio of 1.156. The gain increase that Lo et al. (2015) report for I<E is not reproduced with either input axis. Alternative: that increase is a network effect (the 2013 paper locates the effect in the circuit's energy landscape), which an isolated neuron cannot show. The ratio-dependent shift reproduces the direction of the paper's circuit results (I<E faster, I>E slower) by a different route (net drive rather than gain).
